Dijkstra vs A*

Two pathfinding algorithms solving the same problem on the same real street network, stacked one above the other and started at the same instant. The whole explanation is visual: one of them floods the map in every direction, the other leans toward the target.

RoleDesign & animation
SubjectGraph search
Format1080×1920 vertical
Length11s

The split is the argument

  • Stacked, not side by side. Vertical format means two full-width panels — each map stays large enough to read the street geometry, which a side-by-side split would destroy.
  • Explored cells in cool blue, the found path in red. The blue area is the wasted work. You don't need a narrator to say A* explored less; the top panel is visibly bluer.
  • A real street network, not a grid. Grids make both algorithms look similar. Irregular city topology is where the heuristic actually shows.
  • Labels stay static. Each panel is titled once and never animates, so all motion on screen belongs to the algorithms.

What it is really teaching

Both algorithms return the same shortest path — that is the part people miss. The film is not about one being better; it is about one of them knowing which direction the goal is in. Ending both panels on an identical red route is what makes that land.
