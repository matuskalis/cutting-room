Smart vs Random

A single solver that navigates deliberately against a hundred that move at random, all released into the same maze at once, with a clock running. The outcome isn't in doubt — the interest is in how long the swarm takes and how close it gets by accident.

RoleDesign & animation
Format1080×1920 vertical
Length29s
Contestants1 vs 100

Making an unfair race worth watching

  • Asymmetric colour weight. The lone smart solver is cyan and drawn brighter; the hundred are a warmer, dimmer amber. One dot has to hold attention against a crowd, so it gets the visual advantage.
  • The crowd is the texture. A hundred random walkers produce a shifting cloud that fills the maze — it reads as noise, which is the point being made about undirected search.
  • Clock in the header. A running timer converts an algorithm demo into a sporting event, and gives the ending a number to land on.
  • Scoreboard, not narration. The title bar states the matchup in four words. Nothing else is explained.

Same family, different lesson

This sits next to the Dijkstra vs A* piece deliberately. That one compares two algorithms that both know what they're doing; this one compares thinking with not thinking at all. Together they bracket the same idea from either end.
