Territory Clash

Four coloured factions expand across a shared grid, each following a different rule — one weaves, one grabs open ground, one fortifies, one spirals. A live leaderboard converts what is really a cellular automaton into something you watch like a race.

RoleDesign & animation
Format1080×1920 vertical
Length28s
VariantsMultiple palettes & seeds

Giving the maths a cast

  • Strategies get names and one-line characters. Weaver, Prospector, Guardian, Spiral — each with a short description under its bar. Named things can be rooted for; rule sets cannot.
  • Leaderboard as the second subject. Percentage bars update continuously in the lower third and reorder as positions change, so the outcome is legible even if you look away from the grid.
  • A banner names the current leader. One line at the top, always showing who is ahead — the piece of information the whole clip is built to deliver.
  • Seed and speed shown on screen. Stating the run parameters makes it a recorded experiment rather than an edited outcome.
  • Saturated colours, dark field. Four hues that stay distinguishable when a region is a single cell wide.

Tuning for watchability

The unfun failure mode is one faction snowballing in the first three seconds. Getting a run that stays contested to the end needed rules about walls and how leftover space gets filled, plus per-faction jitter — enough randomness that runs differ, not so much that strategies stop reading as characters. Most of the design work here was in that tuning, not in the frame.
